## 0. What changed from v4, and why

The founder reported that the **"Hit" (On-point) legend pill** on the Play screen always seems to show `+2%` and never varies, while the **"Miss" pill** clearly cycles through its `{-5, -4, -3}` variety. v4 §2 made both pills show the last *actual rolled value* per band.

We investigated and **ruled out a randomization bug.** An empirical 200,000-sample simulation of `lifeDeltaFor` (`lib/features/timing_engine/timing_engine.dart`) confirms the On-point `{2.0, 3.0}` split is a correct, balanced ~50/50. The roll mechanics are fine and stay **exactly as they are** (v3 §4, carried forward).

The real cause is **sampling frequency, not the roll.** The On-point band is currently a narrow 31–80ms window (`perfectMs = 30`, `onPointMs = 80`), while Miss catches everything past 80ms with no upper bound. In real play, genuine On-point taps are rare relative to Misses, so the Hit pill — which only ever shows one of two values and is seeded to `+2%` — almost never receives enough live updates to visibly vary. The Miss pill, fed by the overwhelmingly more common Miss band, updates constantly and shows its full range.

**The fix (founder's chosen direction):** widen the On-point band so genuine on-point hits happen often enough during real play that the `+2%/+3%` variety actually becomes visible.

This is a **one-constant amendment.**

## 1. The decision — `onPointMs`: 80 → 200

**Decision: set `TimingConfig.onPointMs = 200` (up from 80). Leave `perfectMs` at 30, unchanged.**

New bands:

| Band | Before (v4) | After (v5) |
|---|---|---|
| Perfect | \|Δ\| ≤ 30ms | \|Δ\| ≤ 30ms *(unchanged)* |
| On-point | 31–80ms (49ms wide) | 31–200ms (169ms wide) |
| Miss | >80ms (unbounded) | >200ms (unbounded) |

### 1.1 Why 200ms (grounded in the actual task)

The Play task is **interval estimation** — "press when you think N seconds have passed," with N randomized 3–20s and only the "Tap at" text as a cue. This is *not* a rhythm/beat-matching task with an external metronome; there is no anchor to align to, so timing error is dominated by the player's internal clock.

Human interval estimation obeys the **scalar property** (Weber's law): the spread of estimates scales roughly *proportionally* with the interval, with a coefficient of variation typically in the ~5–15% range for supra-second durations. Concretely, that means an expected error standard deviation on the order of:

- **~150–450ms at a 3s target** (the easiest end), and
- **~1000–3000ms at a 20s target** (the hard end).

Against that reality, the old 80ms window is a *sliver* — smaller than one standard deviation of error even at the easiest interval — which is exactly why genuine On-point hits were rare. **200ms is calibrated to be meaningful but not trivial:**

- At a 3s target (SD ≈ 300ms, mid-range assumption), a ±200ms On-point window captures roughly **40% of taps as On-point**, with Perfect (±30ms) still a hard-earned ~8% and Miss the remaining majority. On-point becomes *common enough to vary visibly* — the whole point of the fix — without becoming the default outcome.
- At longer targets the band naturally shrinks as a fraction of the widening error distribution, so Miss reclaims dominance and the game stays harder for long intervals. The difficulty gradient across the 3–20s range is preserved, not flattened.
- 200ms is still **an order of magnitude tighter than the multi-hundred-ms-to-multi-second errors** typical of unaided interval estimation, so it does not trivialize scoring — a careless tap still misses easily.

Why not the alternatives considered: **150ms** captures only ~30% On-point at the easy end — it fixes the visibility problem more marginally and leaves less headroom for slightly-longer intervals where On-point would again get scarce; the founder's concern is *under*-representation, so we bias toward the more generous end. **250ms+** starts to make On-point the *default* even at mid intervals, eroding challenge more than the fix requires. **200ms** is the smallest round value that comfortably guarantees frequent On-point sampling across most of the interval range while keeping Perfect elite and Miss a real threat. It is a clean 2.5× of the current 80.

### 1.2 Why `perfectMs` stays at 30 (only `onPointMs` moves)

The founder's ask was specifically that the **On-point/Hit band is underrepresented.** Perfect is a different tier with a different job: it is the fixed `+3%`, no-roll, guaranteed elite-tolerance reward ceiling (v3 §4.1). Its rarity is *the point* — it is meant to be a rare, skillful outcome, and it does not feed either legend pill (a Perfect tap touches neither pill — v4 §2.1), so it is **irrelevant to the pill-variety problem** we are fixing. Widening it would dilute the elite tier and change game feel in a direction the founder did not ask for. **Leave it tight and meaningful at 30ms.** Only `onPointMs` moves.

## 2. Intended side effect — the game gets more forgiving

This is called out explicitly so it reads as **intentional, not an oversight.** A wider On-point band means more taps that used to land as Miss (a `-5…-3%` life loss) now land as On-point (a `+2…+3%` life gain). Net effect: **runs last longer and scoring is more forgiving overall.** That is an accepted, deliberate consequence of making On-point common enough for the pill to vary — it is not a regression.

It is also fully consistent with the framing already in `timing_config.dart`'s doc comment and the discovery doc: these ms thresholds are **"playtest defaults, not final,"** explicitly expected to be re-tuned on real device feel during the Days 3–5 Gate 1 feel prototype. v5 is exactly that kind of feel-tuning pass. The value is not locked; if 200ms plays too soft on device, the next revision tightens it — that is the expected loop, not a defect.

**No compensating change** to the life-delta magnitudes, Miss range, death threshold, or target-duration range is made in v5. Widening one band is the whole change; stacking more tuning on top would make the effect hard to attribute during playtesting.

## 3. Player-facing readout follows the constant automatically

`play_screen.dart` renders a fixed tolerance readout `'±${TimingConfig.onPointMs}ms'` (currently shows **"±80ms"**). Because it reads the constant directly, it will **automatically display "±200ms"** after this change — **no code edit is required there**, and this is *correct*: the readout should truthfully reflect the wider tolerance the player now has. Flagged so no one is surprised by the on-screen number changing, and so `game-ux-designer` notes the readout copy is now "±200ms" (the design docs' `±80ms` mockup text is historical and not edited in place per convention).
